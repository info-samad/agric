# Evidence for organic manure guidance

Verified by search then fetch on 2026-11-11.

1. USU Extension — Sustainable Manure and Compost Application: Garden and Micro Farm Guidelines
https://extension.usu.edu/yardandgarden/research/sustainable-manure-and-compost-application
Supports variable manure content by animal, feed, bedding, storage, moisture; cattle/sheep/goat/poultry types; poultry often higher salts and nutrients; pathogens E. coli/Listeria/Salmonella; nutrient laboratory testing; P/K accumulation when dosing to N; compost does not eliminate salts and may concentrate salinity; N loss during composting; organic and inorganic N differ in availability. Do not reuse Utah universal rates or nutrient averages for Syrian farms. Official USDA contact classification is preferable to examples in this fact sheet.

2. USDA AMS — Soil Building - Manures & Composts
https://www.ams.usda.gov/grades-standards/soil-building-manures-composts
US organic standard §205.203: raw manure incorporated >=120 days before harvest if edible portion contacts soil (including splash), >=90 days otherwise. These are US organic standards, not Syrian law and not sufficient safety guarantees. Managed compost process: initial C:N 25:1–40:1, 131–170 F for 3 days in vessel/static aerated pile or 15 days windrows with >=5 turns. Raw-manure tea must follow raw manure rules.

3. FAO — On-farm composting methods, 1. Composting process and techniques
https://www.fao.org/4/y5104e/y5104e05.htm
Aerobic composting is controlled microorganism decomposition, needs aeration, moisture and C:N balance. 50–60% starting moisture advised; 40–65% operational range. Active hot phase followed by curing. No reheating after turning, ambient temperature, dark uniform soil-like material are signs, not pathogen test. Immaturity risks N hunger, O deficiency, phytotoxic organic acids. Cold/anaerobic decomposition may leave pathogens and weed seeds.

4. NDSU Extension — Composting Animal Manures: A guide to the process and management of animal manure compost, revised Feb 2022
https://www.ndsu.edu/agriculture/extension/publications/composting-animal-manures-guide-process-and-management-animal-manure-compost
Routine oxygen by turning, moisture hand wrung-rag check, temp probe in multiple positions/depths; C:N balance with plant bulking materials; cure after heating subsides; immature compost risks phytotoxicity and immobilization. Soil and finished compost should be tested, representative mixed samples. Availability from compost differs from raw manure; no universal availability factor. Limit application by crop nutrient needs and prevent phosphorus accumulation; store/compost without runoff reaching water.

5. UMN Extension — Soil health and nutrient management in high tunnels
https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/specialty-crops/soil-health-and-nutrient-management-in-high-tunnels
Verified by search then fetch. Annual soil tests including pH, P/K, organic matter, EC/soluble salts and nitrate. Rain exclusion and narrow drip wetting zone; inputs and irrigation cause salts accumulation. Composted manure linked with high P accumulation; test manure/compost, incorporate rather than leave on surface. Credit residual N, soil N, irrigation nitrate in fertility planning. Only water-soluble suitable fertilizer products for fertigation. Do not transplant Minnesota crop rates to Syria.

6. UMaine Extension — Bulletin #2510, Guidelines for Using Manure on Vegetable Gardens
https://extension.umaine.edu/publications/2510e/
Verified by search then fetch. Pathogens transmission manure to produce; prefer composted manure; never sidedress growing plants with raw manure; incorporate and distribute in soil. Supplemental verification, not included in final five-source records.

7. Oregon State University Extension — Interpreting compost analyses
https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/em-9217-interpreting-compost-analyses
Verified by search then fetch. EC measured by compost 1:5 extract vs saturated extract are not directly interchangeable, dilution changes EC substantially. Total nutrients are not immediately available nutrients; maturity differs from stability; respiration, ammonium/nitrate, germination tests aid evaluation. Dry-stacked poultry litter sold as compost may not be stable. Moisture and dry-matter basis must be distinguished for application mass calculations. Final sources selected: USU, USDA AMS, FAO, UMN, OSU.

Excluded FAQ https://ask.extension.org/kb/faq.php?id=568867: expert explicitly stated no expert available and answer was personal opinion. Do not use as strong evidence for manure tea drip clogging. Precaution against injecting raw manure/tea is based on raw-manure pathogen risks and USDA classification of raw-manure tea, not a claimed universally tested ban on all manufactured organic fertigation products.
